## Targeted search result and priority judgment

Queries combined “identified set,” “minimum candidate dimension,” “bounded solver error,” “monotone threshold crossing,” “interval observations,” and “mixed integer optimization.” We did **not identify an equivalent theorem in the literature audit** for the joint scalar-observation result in `T3_5_PARTIAL_IDENTIFICATION.md`, Theorem C: a valid full lower bound, a vector of feasible incumbent objective values, bounded per-budget optimization error, unknown full optimum, exact nonconvex identified-set membership, and a matching finite-MIO observational-equivalence construction. This is a narrow finding, not a global firstness statement. Broader partial-identification literature makes it quite plausible that its suffix/prefix envelopes and minimax logic are a specialization of established order-restriction techniques.

Likewise, no inspected MIO paper stated Theorem B's exact set of *all possible observed first-pass budgets* under bounded candidate-incumbent error. But once the profile is known, its constructive proof is short; absence in the inspected papers is not sufficient to call it a deep independent advance. Theorem A's general inverse/frontier mechanism has clear analogues in monotone threshold identification and is not a novelty moat by itself.

Before any submission-level priority language, a specialist must search full theorem texts in monotone interval-data/partial-identification research and optimization sensitivity analysis. The manuscript's known misattributed Maragno/Laufer references and incorrect Wada contrast remain deferred to an approved T5 rewrite, as recorded in `T3_PENDING_LITERATURE_FIXES.md`.
